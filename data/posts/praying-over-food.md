So, this question is one that might be kind of unimportant when first looked at but it is actually seen in some very significant parts of scripture.

The question:

> Why do we pray over food and what should we be praying for?

Ok, I think we all know what I am talking about, even non-believers would too. We might call it by a variety of different names such as:

- “saying grace”
- “blessing the meal”
- “giving thanks”
- etc

but honestly… why? And what are we actually supposed to be praying here? Is it just a time to say “hey God, thanks!” or actually pleading some sort of supernatural power to be infused into the food? Some quick Googling turned into a study and here we are. Starting with the direct Scriptures (everything in NKJV unless otherwise noted; text in bold or italics added by me):

> Now the Spirit expressly says that in latter times some will depart from the faith, giving heed to deceiving spirits and doctrines of demons, speaking lies in hypocrisy, having their own conscience seared with a hot iron, forbidding to marry, and commanding to abstain from ***foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving*** by those who believe and know the truth. For every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if it is ***received with thanksgiving***; for it is ***sanctified by the word of God and prayer***. - 1 Timothy 4:1-5 NKJV

Let’s start here since this is where my research started. I am writing this as a process so you may not even see this draft of this article. Paul writes to his disciple, Timothy, warning of people that depart from faith in Jesus Christ and what those people will do and teach. He mentions that they will command people to keep away from food which God created to be received with thanksgiving. He then goes on to point out that every creature (Greek word meaning literally everything created) of God is good and should not be refused (original Greek meaning “should not be thrown away as unclean”)… doesn’t say we have to eat it, more of a “don’t judge those that do” which is exactly what we see in Romans 14 (see below). Do note that I haven’t finished the sentence: it says not to refuse it if it is received with thanksgiving. The sentence finishes with saying that the food is sanctified (Greek literally means to consecrate/dedicate to God or purify) by the word of God and prayer… two methods of sanctification which is interesting and my best analogy right now for it is pilot vs plane. The word of God is like the aircraft. It has everything it needs to fly, it just needs a pilot (you and I) to fly (pray) the word into physical existence. If that is unclear, don’t worry about it, I will have more semi-confusing apologies later which, when all put together, might create a decent picture if you have a strong glasses prescription. Anyways, moving on…

> All things are lawful for me, but not all things are helpful; all things are lawful for me, but not all things edify. Let no one seek his own, but each one the other's well-being. Eat whatever is sold in the meat market, asking no questions for conscience' sake; for "the earth is the LORD's, and all its fullness." If any of those who do not believe invites you to dinner, and you desire to go, eat whatever is set before you, asking no question for conscience' sake. But if anyone says to you, "This was offered to idols," do not eat it for the sake of the one who told you, and for conscience' sake; for "the earth is the LORD's, and all its fullness." "Conscience," I say, not your own, but that of the other. For why is my liberty judged by another man's conscience? But if I ***partake with thanks***, why am I evil spoken of for ***the food over which I give thanks***? Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, ***do all to the glory of God***. - 1 Corinthians 10:23-31 NKJV

A bunch of interesting things here but I am going to focus on the patter that we can already start to see from this 1 Cor. passage compared to the 1 Tim. passage: the giving of thanks or thanksgiving. The word in the Greek literally means to be thankful, so, no hidden meaning here. 

> Receive one who is weak in the faith, but not to disputes over doubtful things. For one believes he may eat all things, but he who is weak eats only vegetables. Let not him who eats despise him who does not eat, and let not him who does not eat judge him who eats; for God has received him. Who are you to judge another's servant? To his own master he stands or falls. Indeed, he will be made to stand, for God is able to make him stand. One person esteems one day above another; another esteems every day alike. Let each be fully convinced in his own mind. He who observes the day, observes it to the Lord; and he who does not observe the day, to the Lord he does not observe it. He who eats, eats to the Lord, for ***he gives God thanks***; and he who does not eat, to the Lord he does not eat, and ***gives God thanks.*** - Romans 14:1-6 NKJV
> And when he had said these things, he took bread and ***gave thanks to God*** in the presence of them all; and when he had broken it he began to eat. - Acts 27:35 NKJV
> And Jesus took the loaves, and when ***He had given thanks*** He distributed them to the disciples, and the disciples to those sitting down; and likewise of the fish, as much as they wanted. - John 6:11 NKJV
> And He took the seven loaves and the fish and ***gave thanks***, broke them and gave them to His disciples; and the disciples gave to the multitude. - Matthew 15:36 NKJV
> And He took bread, ***gave thanks*** and broke it, and gave it to them, saying, "This is My body which is given for you; do this in remembrance of Me." - Luke 22:19 NKJV
> Then He took the cup, and when ***He had given thanks*** He gave it to them, and they all drank from it. - Mark 14:23 NKJV
